Unit 1: Rotational Motion of a Rigid Solid Around a Fixed Axis 7 Hours
Prerequisites:
Motion relativity, average/instantaneous speed, trajectory, moment of a force.
Problem Situation:
Doors and bicycle wheels rotate around fixed axles. How is rotation around a fixed axis characterized, and what links angular speed to linear speed?
ObjectivesContentsDidactic MaterialsActivities & Evaluation
  • Define rotational motion around a fixed axis.
  • Define angular position θ(t) and curvilinear abscissa s(t) = R·θ.
  • Average and instantaneous angular velocity ω = dθ/dt (rad/s).
  • Relation between linear speed and angular velocity: v = R · ω.
  • Uniform circular rotation: period T = 2π/ω, frequency f = 1/T.
  • Time equation of uniform rotation: θ(t) = ω·t + θ₀.
 I. Position of Point on Rotating Body (1. Fixed axis rotation, 2. Angular & curvilinear position)
II. Angular Velocity (1. Average angular speed, 2. Instantaneous angular speed, 3. Relation v = R·ω)
III. Uniform Rotational Motion (1. Definition, 2. Period & frequency, 3. Time equation θ(t))
  • Air cushion table with rotating disc accessory
  • Spark timer, non-elastic thread, pulleys
  • Metric circular board, chronometer, Avimeca software
 Activities: Record trajectory of points at various radii; verify that v/R = ω is constant for all points. Write the time equation.
Evaluation: Angular velocity calculations, time equation exercises, Exam 1.

Unit 2: Work and Power of a Force 6 Hours
Prerequisites:
Forces, translation/rotation motion, dot product of vectors.
Problem Situation:
We commonly say someone does more work by lifting heavy loads. What is mechanical work in physics, and how is power evaluated?
ObjectivesContentsDidactic MaterialsActivities & Evaluation
  • Work of constant force: W_AB(F) = F · AB · cos(α) (Joules).
  • Motoring work (W > 0), resistive work (W < 0), zero work.
  • Work of weight: W_AB(P) = ± m·g·h (path-independent).
  • Work of torque in rotation: W = M_Δ · Δθ.
  • Average power P_m = W / Δt and instantaneous power P = F · v.
 I. Work of Forces (1. Constant force in translation, 2. Work of weight, 3. Constant torque in rotation)
II. Power of Forces (1. Average power, 2. Instantaneous power in translation P = F·v, 3. Instantaneous power in rotation P = M·ω)
  • Inclined plane bench, pulleys, dynamometers
  • Masses, strings, air track with gliders
  • Winch apparatus for rotational work measurement
 Activities: Demonstrate that work of weight depends only on vertical elevation h. Compute power of electric winches.
Evaluation: Work calculation problems on inclined planes.
Unit 3: Work and Kinetic Energy 5 Hours
Prerequisites:
Mechanical work, velocity, moment of inertia.
Problem Situation:
A blender knife rotates faster and grinds food more rapidly because of acquired kinetic energy. How is kinetic energy related to the sum of work done on a solid?
ObjectivesContentsDidactic MaterialsActivities & Evaluation
  • Kinetic energy in translation: E_c = 0.5 · m · v² (Joules).
  • Kinetic energy in rotation: E_c = 0.5 · J_Δ · ω².
  • Moment of inertia J_Δ in kg·m².
  • Kinetic Energy Theorem: ΔE_c = E_c2 - E_c1 = Σ W_1→2(F_ext).
  • Apply theorem to free fall, inclined planes, and rotating solids.
 I. Kinetic Energy (1. Solid in translation, 2. Solid in rotation)
II. Kinetic Energy Theorem (1. Free fall case, 2. Rectilinear translation case, 3. Rotation case, 4. Statement)
III. Applications (1. Friction evaluation, 2. Speed determination)
  • Free fall apparatus with digital optical timer
  • Air track with photogates and hanging load
  • Flywheel with cord and weights for rotational study
 Activities: Verify experimentally that 0.5·m·v² = m·g·h in free fall. Measure terminal velocity on inclined plane.
Evaluation: Kinetic energy theorem problem solving and Supervised Exam 2.

Unit 4: Work and Gravitational Potential Energy 5 Hours
Prerequisites:
Work of weight, elevation, reference state.
Problem Situation:
Water stored behind high dams stores vast energy called gravitational potential energy. How is this energy expressed and utilized?
ObjectivesContentsDidactic MaterialsActivities & Evaluation
  • Understand gravitational potential energy E_pp.
  • Formula: E_pp = m · g · z + C (Joules).
  • Choose a reference state (E_pp = 0 at z = 0).
  • Fundamental relation: ΔE_pp = -W_AB(P) = m·g·(z_B - z_A).
 I. Gravitational Potential Energy (1. Definition, 2. Formula E_pp = m·g·z + C, 3. Reference state)
II. Variation of Potential Energy (1. Relation ΔE_pp = -W(P), 2. Applications)
  • Textbook, board, computer media
  • Stands, vertical scale, steel balls
 Activities: Establish formula E_pp = m·g·z by integration of weight work. Show that choice of reference changes C but not ΔE_pp.
Evaluation: Potential energy calculations with various reference planes.
